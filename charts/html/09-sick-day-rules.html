Sick Day Rules

Illness raises stress hormones and can spike glucose — or cause lows if you can’t eat. Have a plan before you need it.

Do these every sick day
  1. Take basal insulin / usual meds unless told otherwise.
  2. Check glucose every 2–4 hours (more if rising).
  3. Check ketones if glucose ≥250 or as instructed.
  4. Sip fluids often — aim for hydration.
  5. Rest; ask someone to check on you.
Call your care team if
  • Vomiting or diarrhea lasting >4–6 hours
  • Moderate/large ketones
  • Glucose stays >250–300 despite plan
  • Signs of dehydration
  • Fever with confusion or rapid breathing
  • You can’t keep fluids down
  • You’re unsure about medication doses
Carb & fluid ideas when appetite is low
~15g options
  • ½ cup juice
  • ½ cup regular gelatin
  • 6 saltines
  • ½ cup applesauce
  • 1 cup sports drink**
Hydration
  • Water, broth
  • Electrolyte drinks
  • Ice chips / popsicles
  • Sugar-free fluids if glucose high**
Medicine notes
  • Know which OTC meds are OK
  • Sugar-free ≠ carb-free always
  • SGLT2 users: ask about ketoacidosis risk when ill
  • Never stop insulin abruptly

**Choose sugared vs sugar-free fluids based on glucose and your sick-day plan.

Type 1 / insulin users Illness can trigger DKA even if you’re not eating. Continue basal insulin. Check ketones. Follow your prescribed sick-day insulin adjustments.
Emergency now Severe abdominal pain, fruity breath, deep breathing, lethargy, or inability to keep any fluids down — seek emergency care.
