Insulin Types & Timing

Knowing onset, peak, and duration helps you plan meals, corrections, and overnight coverage. Brand names vary — match this chart to what your clinician prescribed.

Action profile overview
Type Examples* Onset Peak Duration
Rapid Lispro, aspart, glulisine 10–20 min 1–3 hr 3–5 hr
Short / R Regular (U-100) 30–60 min 2–4 hr 5–8 hr
Intermediate NPH 1–3 hr 4–8 hr 12–16 hr
Long Glargine, detemir 1–2 hr Minimal ~20–24 hr
Ultra-long Degludec, U-300 glargine ~1 hr Minimal 36–42+ hr

*Examples of classes — not a prescribing list. Timing varies by dose, site, and person.

Common roles
  • Bolus (meal/correction): rapid or short-acting
  • Basal (background): long or ultra-long; sometimes NPH
  • Premixed: fixed basal + bolus ratios
  • Pump / AID: rapid-acting for basal & bolus
Safety reminders
  • Never share pens or needles
  • Store unopened insulin as labeled
  • Track in-use pen expiry dates
  • Rotate injection sites
  • Clarify cloudy vs clear products before injecting
Stacking risk Giving another correction before the previous rapid insulin has peaked can cause delayed hypoglycemia. Follow your clinician’s correction spacing (often ~3–4 hours) and “insulin on board” guidance.

Educational reference only — not medical advice. Insulin regimens are individualized. Do not change doses without clinician direction.
