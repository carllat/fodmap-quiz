Daily Foot Care Checklist

Diabetes can reduce sensation and slow healing. Small blisters or cracks can become serious infections. A two-minute daily habit protects mobility.

Every day
  • Wash feet with lukewarm water & mild soap
  • Dry carefully — especially between toes
  • Inspect tops, soles, heels, and between toes
  • Use a mirror or ask for help if needed
  • Apply lotion to tops & bottoms (not between toes)
  • Wear clean, dry socks (seamless if possible)
  • Check shoes for pebbles, nails, rough lining
  • Never walk barefoot indoors or outdoors
  • Trim nails straight across; file sharp edges
  • Note any redness, warmth, swelling, or drainage
Safe habits
  • Test water temperature with your elbow/wrist
  • Choose well-fitting shoes; break in gradually
  • Alternate pairs of shoes when you can
  • Keep blood flow moving — wiggle toes, ankle circles
  • Don’t smoke — it worsens circulation
Avoid these
  • Heating pads / hot water bottles on feet
  • Medicated corn removers / razor scrapers
  • Tight socks with restrictive tops
  • Crossing legs for long periods
  • Ignoring “minor” cuts or blisters
Call your clinician promptly for
  • New ulcer, blister, or crack
  • Puncture wound
  • Ingrown toenail
  • Red streaks or pus
  • Increasing pain or swelling
  • Color / temperature change
  • Foot fungus that worsens
  • Numbness that’s new
  • Any wound not improving in 1–2 days
Annual professional check Ask for a comprehensive foot exam at least once a year (more often with neuropathy, prior ulcers, or poor circulation). Podiatry referral can prevent serious complications.

Educational reference only — not medical advice. Seek urgent care for rapidly worsening infection, fever with a foot wound, or black/blue tissue changes.
